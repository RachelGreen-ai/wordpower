# Speaker Mining Content Strategy

This is the long-term content method for the Professional English series.

The core shift: do not start from topics. Start from people worth learning from.

## 1. Discover High-quality Speakers

Speaker sources can include:

- Public company leaders, founders, investors, operators, and researchers
- Celebrity interviews where the person has unusually strong conversation judgment
- YouTube channels with consistently intelligent interviews, talks, or explainers
- Conference talks, keynote speeches, podcasts, long-form interviews, and career-learning videos

Selection criteria:

- The speaker has a distinct English personality, not just good information.
- Their speech shows judgment, restraint, humor, clarity, courage, or emotional intelligence.
- Their content has repeatable patterns learners can practice.
- The material is recent or culturally relevant enough to feel alive.
- The content can teach at least two layers: language and thinking.

Avoid:

- Generic productivity advice with no distinctive voice
- Viral clips that do not survive deeper analysis
- Hype-heavy AI talks with no operational detail
- Content that only teaches vocabulary but not judgment

## 2. Deep-dive the Person

For each promising speaker, build a small speaker dossier before writing lessons.

Required notes:

- Why this person is worth studying
- What kind of English personality they model
- Their recurring themes and favorite frames
- Their answer structure in interviews or talks
- Their best reusable phrases
- Their leadership signals: how they disagree, explain uncertainty, set boundaries, or inspire trust
- Their emotional tone: calm, sharp, warm, strategic, rebellious, reflective, humorous
- Which learner persona benefits from studying them

Speaker archetypes:

- The calm operator: precise, responsible, measured
- The visionary builder: energetic, future-facing, narrative-driven
- The principled contrarian: clear, boundary-setting, independent
- The reflective celebrity: emotionally intelligent, human, memorable
- The teacher-explainer: structured, generous, concrete
- The career mentor: practical, encouraging, confidence-building

## 3. Extract What To Learn

Each source should be analyzed through four lenses.

### Language

- Signature phrases
- Useful verbs and nouns
- Natural transitions
- Soft disagreement
- Framing sentences
- Interview answers
- Demo narration

### Thinking

- How the speaker scopes a question
- How they separate evidence from opinion
- How they handle uncertainty
- How they make tradeoffs visible
- How they turn stories into principles

### Leadership

- How they sound credible without overclaiming
- How they build trust
- How they admit limits
- How they create alignment
- How they make a complex topic feel manageable

### English Personality

- What kind of self this English produces
- How the learner might sound if they absorb this style
- Which parts should be imitated directly
- Which parts should be adapted to the learner's own voice

## 4. Transform Into Lessons

Every final lesson should answer:

- What moment from the talk or interview are we learning from?
- What is the transferable language move?
- What is the thinking move behind the language?
- What can a learner say in their own professional life?
- What drill makes the learner practice the move actively?

Lesson format:

- Source: speaker, channel, video or article URL
- Why this source matters
- Talk flow or interview flow
- Phrase bank
- Vocabulary that carries judgment, not just meaning
- Logic moves
- Leadership lines
- Practice drill

## 5. Series Architecture

Current tracks:

- `customer-facing-ai`: AI consulting, FDE, solution architecture, stakeholder language
- `celebrity-talks`: public figures, long-form interviews, conversation intelligence
- `learning-career`: career change, job search, learning strategy, professional self-narration

Future speaker-based collections can sit inside these tracks:

- "Learn from Google Cloud speakers"
- "Learn from great interviewers"
- "Learn from founders explaining hard things"
- "Learn from celebrities with emotional intelligence"
- "Learn from career mentors"
- "Learn from technical explainers"

## 6. Content Quality Bar

A lesson is strong only if it teaches all three:

- English: better words, phrases, and sentence rhythm
- Judgment: better distinctions, boundaries, and reasoning
- Persona: a more capable way to sound in English

If a source only gives information, it belongs in research notes.
If a source gives language plus judgment plus persona, it can become a lesson.

## 7. Operating Loop

For each batch:

1. Find 8-12 candidate speakers or channels.
2. Shortlist 3-5 based on the quality bar.
3. Build speaker dossiers.
4. Pick the best 1-3 sources per speaker.
5. Extract notes before writing lessons.
6. Create lessons only after the learning angle is clear.
7. Add SEO/GEO summaries after the lesson is meaningful.
